## Problem

Shiritori (しりとり) is the word-chain game every Japanese kid knows. Say a word, and the next word must start with the last kana of yours. End on ん and you lose.

It's also a great vocabulary drill for learners. But most digital versions are built for native speakers: they assume a Japanese keyboard, give no help when you're stuck, and punish mistakes harshly.

## Users

- **Japanese learners** who know a few hundred words and want to use them.
- **People without a Japanese IME**, typing on a normal keyboard or a phone.

## Constraints

- **The rules are subtle.** Small kana (しゃ → や), long vowels (コーヒー), voiced variants (は / ば / ぱ), and katakana all need rules that feel fair.
- **Input is messy.** Romaji, katakana, full-width characters, trailing spaces.
- **Free to run.** A static web app, no servers to babysit.

## Key decisions and trade-offs

<Tradeoff
  chose="Accept romaji and katakana and normalize everything to hiragana"
  over="Requiring a Japanese keyboard"
  because="Learners shouldn't lose a round to their keyboard. Normalizing input made the game playable on any device."
/>

<Tradeoff
  chose="Lenient matching of voiced / unvoiced kana"
  over="Strict native-speaker rules"
  because="Beginners get stuck on dakuten edge cases. A slightly friendlier rule keeps the game about vocabulary, not technicalities."
/>

## What I built

- **Game logic**: turn validation, used-word tracking, ん detection, and the computer's replies.
- **Word verification system**: every word is checked against JLPT vocabulary from an online API, so only real words count.
- **UI**: a fast, friendly single screen with clear feedback on every move.
